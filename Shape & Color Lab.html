Shape & Color Lab

Color and geometry share one idea: the circle. Hue is an angle, and every regular shape is a way of cutting a circle into equal turns. Move the sliders and watch the two lock together.

1. Sides become hues

A regular polygon with n sides has corners every 360° ÷ n. Put those corners on the color wheel and each corner lands on a hue. Two sides give complementary colors, three give a triad, four give a square tetrad.

2. One turning angle, two patterns

Place dot k at a distance √k from the center, turned k × a degrees. Color each dot by that same turning angle. A divisor of 360 (120°, 90°) stacks dots into spokes and a few repeating colors. An irrational-feeling angle like the golden angle, 137.5°, never repeats, so dots pack evenly and every hue appears, as in sunflower seeds.

Things to notice

  • Hue is circular: 0° and 360° are the same red, which is why a wheel works and a straight bar does not.
  • Rotating the polygon (start hue) changes every color but keeps the relationships between them.
  • Saturation is distance from the center of a color disc; lightness is height. Together with hue they form a cylinder, a 3D shape.
  • In section 2, try 144° (pentagram, 5 spokes) and 72° (also 5 spokes). Angles that share a divisor with 360 give the same spoke count.
